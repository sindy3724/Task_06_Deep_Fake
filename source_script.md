Coach, here's what the workload data is telling us heading into week nine.

Over the last four weeks, three starters have logged an average of 620 high-intensity distance meters per session, roughly eighteen percent above their in-season baseline. That spike lines up almost exactly with the soft-tissue complaints we've seen in the training room. It's not a coincidence — acute-to-chronic workload ratios for all three players crossed above 1.5 in back-to-back weeks, and that threshold is where the injury literature consistently starts flashing yellow.

The good news is this is fixable without sacrificing form. I'd recommend three specific changes.

First, cap high-intensity distance in training to eighty-five percent of match-day load for these three players through the bye week. That alone should bring the acute-to-chronic ratio back under 1.3 within ten days, based on how quickly it climbed in the first place.

Second, build one additional low-intensity recovery day into the microcycle. Right now the rotation gives them one full rest day and five training days at moderate-to-high intensity. Shifting to two lighter days doesn't cost you a training session, it just changes what that session asks of their legs.

Third, and this is the one I'd push hardest on: stagger their high-intensity days so no two of the three overlap. Right now all three hit their hardest session on Tuesday, which means the medical staff sees three players needing the same recovery resources at the same time. Staggering it spreads that load on both the players and the training room.

None of this requires benching anyone or changing the game plan. It's a load-management adjustment, not a talent decision. If we make these three changes and the numbers hold, I'd expect the acute-to-chronic ratios to normalize by week eleven, which gets everyone healthy well before the postseason stretch.

I can pull the week-by-week breakdown if you want to walk through it with the training staff directly.
